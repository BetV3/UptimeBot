It’s **close**, but I would **change it**.

Not because the numbers are absurd. Because the **packaging is muddled** and the **$49 plan is underpriced for what you’re promising**.

## What is wrong

### 1. Pro says “Unlimited sites” but only 50 monitors

That is sloppy.

If I can have unlimited sites but only 50 monitors, the real limit is monitors, not sites. So “unlimited sites” sounds like marketing fluff. That weakens trust immediately.

A cleaner version is:

* **Up to 10 sites**
* or **Unlimited sites, shared across 50 monitors**
* or just remove site limits entirely and price by monitors only

Right now it reads like you are trying to sound bigger than the plan actually is.

### 2. “Branded status pages per client” at $49 is too generous

That is the biggest pricing mistake.

If you’re targeting agencies, **per-client branded status pages** are not some cute bonus. That is a high-value agency feature. Better Stack charges extra for additional public status pages and even more for white-labeling and advanced status-page options. ([Better Stack][1])

So bundling “branded status pages per client” into a $49 top tier is probably underpricing your best buyer.

### 3. Free might be okay, but it is not strategically sharp

Your free tier is not crazy, but it sits awkwardly in the market:

* Better Stack gives **10 monitors and 1 status page** on free. ([Better Stack][1])
* UptimeRobot gives **50 monitors**, **5-minute intervals**, and **basic status pages** on free. ([Uptime Robot][2])
* Oh Dear does **not** do a permanent free tier at all. It gives a **10-day free trial** and then charges for all features. ([Oh Dear][3])

So your free tier is less generous than some free competitors, which is fine **if** your positioning is strong and your paid plans are clearer. But if your free tier is smaller **and** your paid packaging is fuzzy, you get the worst of both worlds.

### 4. Starter is decent, but custom domain may be too early

At **$12/month**, Starter is actually competitive:

* Checkly has a **Starter** tier at **$12/month billed annually** and **$24/month billed monthly**. ([Checkly][4])
* UptimeRobot Solo sits in the rough **$7 to $19/month** range depending on monitor count, with **60-second checks**, DNS, SSL/domain monitoring, and basic status pages. ([Uptime Robot][2])

So $12 is not the issue. The issue is whether **custom domain** belongs there. That feels like a feature you should use to pull people into Pro, not hand out in the lower paid tier.

## What is actually true

Your pricing is **fine for beta / early validation**, but it is **not well structured for long-term agency monetization**.

The underlying problem is this:

* **Free** is okay for sampling
* **Starter** is okay for solo operators
* **Pro** is trying to be both “serious user” and “agency plan”

That is too much work for one tier.

Agencies are your highest-value buyer. So your plan structure should make that obvious.
